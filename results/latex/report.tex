\begin{abstract}
We study how small perturbations grow in gas that is \emph{cooling}, i.e.\ with no
heating, in contrast to \citet{das21}, where heating balances cooling on average.
The setup has no initial density contrast: a uniform $10^6$\,K medium in which
radiative cooling is switched on in a region of length $L$ (a ``cloud''), or
everywhere (the infinite medium, $L\to\infty$). About 340 one-dimensional gPLUTO
simulations are compared with a linear theory integrated along the cooling
background, for three cooling models (an approximate collisional-equilibrium
curve with a $10^4$\,K floor, Cloudy collisional equilibrium with the same floor,
and Cloudy in the \citealt{hm12} UV background) and five kinds of seed
perturbation (density, temperature, sound wave, velocity, metallicity). The
growth is controlled by $x=\lambda/(\cstc)$ and $L/(\cstc)$. Per e-fold of
cooling, a perturbation grows by as many e-folds as a perturbation in a heated
medium grows per cooling time; but a cooling gas has only $\approx4$ e-folds of
temperature to traverse, so the amplification is finite ($\lesssim10^2$ in an
infinite medium). Much of it happens after the gas has cooled. After that the
perturbation becomes a sound wave in the cold gas, which in the UV background
damps away.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Configurations}

\paragraph{Cloud ($\chi=1$).}
The domain is filled with uniform gas of density $\rho_0$ ($n=\rho_0/m_p$) and
temperature $T_0=10^6$\,K. In a central region of length $L$, the ``cloud'',
radiative cooling is switched on; outside it the gas neither cools nor is heated.
There is \emph{no initial density or temperature contrast}: in the notation of
the earlier drafts, $\chi$ (the initial cloud-to-background density ratio) is 1.
The seed perturbation is applied only inside the cloud. As the cloud cools it
loses pressure support, and the surrounding hot gas flows in and compresses it.
The model depends only on $L/(\cstc)$, $\lambda/(\cstc)$, the metallicity $Z$,
the seed, and (with a UV background) weakly on $n$.

\paragraph{Infinite medium.}
A periodic box exactly one wavelength long, all of it cooling. This is the
$L\to\infty$ limit of the cloud. It has no edges and is the cleanest place to
measure growth.

\paragraph{Relation to the student's setup.} In the student's runs the cloud
started slightly denser than the background ($\chi\approx1.3$ judging from the
poster's temperature profiles) and an \texttt{if} statement switched cooling on
for that gas. $\chi=1$ is the limit with no contrast, which removes $\chi$ as a
parameter. Earlier drafts also showed $\chi=1.3$, 2 and 5; those results are
in the git history (commit \texttt{9623051}) and behave in the same way once
$\lambda$ and $L$ are measured in units of $\cstc$ of the cloud gas.

\subsection{Which gas cools}
\label{sec:switch}
A passive tracer is set to 1 in the cloud and 0 outside, and the cooling rate in
every cell is multiplied by the tracer. The tracer is advected with the gas, so
the switch follows the cloud \emph{material}: the cooling region moves inward
with the cloud as it is compressed, hot gas that flows in behind it does not
cool, and cloud gas keeps cooling even where it becomes underdense. (A switch
based on a density threshold would instead let compressed hot gas start
cooling.) The only departure from an exactly Lagrangian switch is numerical
mixing at the cloud edge; it is quantified in Section~\ref{sec:mixing}.

\subsection{Cooling models}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{CIE + floor (original):} $\Lambda(T,Z)$ = primordial
  collisional-equilibrium cooling from the \citet{katz96} rates plus $Z$ times a
  smooth approximation to the \citet{sd93} solar metal curve, with a floor at
  $\Tfl=10^4$\,K. Compared with Cloudy it is 0.1--0.3\,dex too high between
  $2\times10^5$ and $3\times10^6$\,K.
\item \emph{Cloudy CIE + floor:} the Cloudy collisional-equilibrium tables
  distributed with Grackle \citep{smith17}, with the same floor.
\item \emph{UV background:} the Cloudy photoionization-equilibrium tables for the
  \citet{hm12} background at $z=0$, including photoheating and with no floor. Gas
  settles at $\Teq=1.9\times10^4$, $1.3\times10^4$ and $1.0\times10^4$\,K at
  $n=10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$\,cm$^{-3}$.
\end{enumerate}
Figures~\ref{fig:cooling} and~\ref{fig:coolmodels} show the curves.

\subsection{Seeds and diagnostics}
Unless stated otherwise the seed is $\drho = A\cos(2\pi x/\lambda)$ at uniform
pressure (the denser gas is cooler), with $A=0.02$ in the cloud runs.
Section~\ref{sec:seeds} adds four other seeds. Perturbations are measured by a
least-squares projection onto $\cos(k_m m)$ and $\sin(k_m m)$ in the
\emph{Lagrangian (mass) coordinate} $m$, using only the inner 60\% of the cloud
mass so that the edges are excluded. The same projection gives $\delta T/T$,
$\delta p/p$, the entropy $\delta\ln K$ with $K=p/\rho^\gamma$, the velocity and
the metallicity. The growth factor $G$ is the amplitude of the seeded mode
divided by its initial amplitude.

\subsection{Time normalization}
\label{sec:tnorm}
All times are in units of $\tcool$, defined as \textbf{the time for the
unperturbed gas to cool at constant density to $1.5\,\Tfl$ or $1.5\,\Teq$}.
``Growth while cooling'' means the maximum of $|G|$ for $t\le\tcool$; ``growth
by $2.5\,\tcool$'' includes the post-cooling phase. For $n=10^{-2}$\,cm$^{-3}$,
$T=10^6$\,K and $Z=0.3$ the gas reaches $1.5\times10^4$\,K after 20\,Myr. The
length unit $\cstc$, used for $x=\lambda/(\cstc)$ and $L/(\cstc)$, is instead
built from the \emph{initial} e-folding time $p/[(\gamma-1)n_en_H\Lambda]$ (34\,Myr
in this example) and the initial sound speed (150\,km\,s$^{-1}$), giving
$\cstc=5.1$\,kpc at $n=10^{-2}$\,cm$^{-3}$ and $\propto1/n$ for collisional cooling.

\subsection{Numerical setup and run inventory}
\label{sec:runs}
gPLUTO \citep{mignone07}, 1D Cartesian, HLLC Riemann solver, piecewise-linear
reconstruction, RK2 time stepping, CFL number 0.4, $\gamma=5/3$, tabulated
cooling integrated as an operator-split source term with the time step limited
to a 5\% pressure change per step. Code units are 1\,kpc, 100\,km\,s$^{-1}$
(time unit 9.78\,Myr) and $m_p$. Cloud runs use a uniform grid over a box three
times the cloud length with outflow boundaries; infinite-medium runs use 512
cells over one wavelength with periodic boundaries. Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists
every set; each figure caption repeats the resolution of the runs it shows.

\section{Linear theory on a cooling background}
\label{sec:linear}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Equations}
We follow one Fourier mode in the Lagrangian mass coordinate $m$ on a uniform
background that cools. Write
$\drho = s(t)\cos(k_m m)$, $\delta p/p = q(t)\cos(k_m m)$ and
$\delta v = w(t)\sin(k_m m)$, with physical wavenumber $k=k_m\rho_0$. Let
$D=d\ln\rho_0/dt$, let $L(\rho,T)$ be the net radiative loss per unit volume,
$r=(\gamma-1)L/p_0$, and $L_\rho=\partial L/\partial\ln\rho$,
$L_T=\partial L/\partial\ln T$, $L_Z=\partial L/\partial\ln Z$. Then
\begin{align}
\frac{ds}{dt} &= sD - kw, \label{eq:s}\\
\frac{dw}{dt} &= k\,\frac{p_0}{\rho_0}\,q, \label{eq:w}\\
\frac{dq}{dt} &= qr + \gamma sD - \gamma kw
  - \frac{\gamma-1}{p_0}\left[L_\rho s + L_T(q-s)\right]
  - \frac{\gamma-1}{p_0}L_Z\zeta, \label{eq:q}
\end{align}
where $\zeta=\delta Z/Z$ is a metallicity perturbation, constant on each fluid
element. For collisional cooling $L_\rho=2L$ and $L_T=\LT L$. The perturbation
equations are integrated together with the background equations,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\ln\rho_0}{dt}=D,\qquad \frac{dp_0}{dt}=\gamma p_0D-(\gamma-1)L,
\label{eq:bg}
\end{equation}
using the same cooling tables and interpolation as gPLUTO. The equations are
linear in the \emph{perturbation} only; the background itself is followed
exactly through its whole (very nonlinear) cooling history, from $10^6$\,K to
the floor.

\subsection{The two backgrounds}
\label{sec:backgrounds}
The background must be specified by choosing $D(t)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Isochoric background ($D=0$)}: the density stays fixed and the
  pressure falls as the gas cools. This is the infinite medium, and the interior of
  a cloud much larger than $\cstc$, which has no time to be compressed.
\item \textbf{Isobaric background}: we impose $dp_0/dt=0$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:bg}),
  i.e.\ $D=(\gamma-1)L/(\gamma p_0)=r/\gamma$. The density rises as the gas cools,
  $\rho_0T_0=$ const. The mode is fixed in the mass coordinate, so its physical
  wavelength shrinks as $\lambda\propto1/\rho_0$ ($k=k_m\rho_0$ grows). This
  describes a cloud much smaller than $\cstc$: sound crosses it quickly, so it stays
  at the pressure of the surrounding non-cooling gas, which keeps its initial
  pressure because it does not cool. The background is uniform; the cloud edges
  and the inflow of hot gas are not modelled. Nothing is imposed in the
  simulations: a $\chi=1$ cloud with $L\ll\cstc$ is compressed in this way on its
  own.
\end{itemize}
The isobaric limit requires $\lambda<L\ll\cstc$ \emph{throughout} the cooling,
which is restrictive. Along an isobaric track $L\propto1/\rho_0\propto T$,
$c_s\propto T^{1/2}$ and $\tcool\propto T^2/\Lambda$, so
$L/(\cstc)\propto\Lambda(T)/T^{3/2}$, which grows by a factor 130 between
$10^6$ and $10^5$\,K and up to $10^3$ near $2\times10^4$\,K. A cloud with
$L/(\cstc)_0=0.1$ therefore stays isobaric only down to $\sim4\times10^5$\,K, and
then cools more nearly isochorically. Real small clouds lie between the two
limits (Section~\ref{sec:clouds}).

\subsection{Limits}
\paragraph{Short wavelength.} Pressure equalizes quickly ($q\to0$) and
\begin{equation}
\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{2-\LT}{\gamma\,\tcool(t)}\,s,\qquad
\tcool(t)\equiv\frac{p_0}{(\gamma-1)L}.
\label{eq:isobaric}
\end{equation}
This is the same instantaneous rate as the isobaric mode of \citet{das21}.
Integrating it along the cooling background gives a closed form: a short-wavelength
perturbation is a \emph{time shift} of the cooling history (the denser parcel
reaches each temperature a little earlier), with
\begin{equation}
\frac{s(T)}{s_0}=\left[\left(\frac{T_0}{T}\right)^2\frac{\Lambda(T)}{\Lambda(T_0)}\right]^{\beta},
\qquad \beta=\begin{cases}1 & \text{isobaric background (small cloud)},\\
1/\gamma & \text{isochoric background (infinite medium)}.\end{cases}
\label{eq:timeshift}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Long wavelength.} The density is frozen ($s$ constant) while
\begin{equation}
\frac{dq}{dt}=\frac{(1-\LT)\,q-(2-\LT)\,s}{\tcool}.
\label{eq:isochoric}
\end{equation}
The temperature and pressure perturbation is driven by the frozen density
perturbation, and grows on its own wherever $\LT<1$. In the heated medium of
\citet{das21} the corresponding condition is $\LT<0$.

\subsection{Comparison with a heated medium}
\label{sec:whyheated}
In the heated medium of \citet{das21} the background is steady: heating balances
cooling, $\tcool$ stays at its initial value $t_{\rm cool,0}$, and a linear
short-wavelength mode grows exponentially at the constant rate
$\sigma_0=(2-\LT)/(\gamma t_{\rm cool,0})$, i.e.\ $G=\exp(\sigma_0 t)$, for as long
as it stays linear.

A cooling gas is different in kind, not just in rate. The perturbation is still
linear, but the background is not steady: its temperature falls by a factor of
$\sim70$, a change that is itself far from small. The instantaneous rate is the
same formula, Eq.~(\ref{eq:isobaric}), evaluated at the \emph{current} state, and
$\tcool$ collapses as the gas cools (Table~\ref{tab:sigma}). Elapsed time is
therefore not a useful clock. The natural clock is the temperature. On an
isochoric background $d\ln T/dt=-1/\tcool$, so Eq.~(\ref{eq:isobaric}) becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\ln s}{d\ln(1/T)}=\frac{2-\LT}{\gamma}
\qquad\Bigl(\text{isobaric background: }2-\LT\Bigr),
\label{eq:perefold}
\end{equation}
which integrates to Eq.~(\ref{eq:timeshift}). \textbf{Per e-fold of cooling, the
perturbation gains $(2-\LT)/\gamma$ e-folds: exactly the number of e-folds a
perturbation in the heated medium gains per cooling time.} Locally the two media
are equally unstable. What differs is the budget: the heated medium can keep
growing for as many cooling times as it lasts, whereas the cooling gas has only
$\ln(10^6/1.5\times10^4)\approx4.2$ e-folds of temperature to traverse, after which
it stops cooling. That is why the amplification of cooling gas is finite and is
set by the shape of $\Lambda(T)$, not by time.

For this example the cooling gas gains $N=4.9$ e-folds (a factor $\approx130$)
over the 4.2 e-folds of cooling from $10^6$ to $1.5\times10^4$\,K, which takes
20\,Myr. A heated medium held at $10^6$\,K gains the same 4.9 e-folds in
$3.5\,t_{\rm cool,0}=118$\,Myr; it gains only 0.84 e-folds in 20\,Myr. Comparing
the two at equal elapsed time, as an earlier draft did, therefore mostly measures
how fast $\tcool$ collapses, not a difference in the instability.

\paragraph{Linear in which variable?} ``Linear'' requires both $|\drho|\ll1$ and
$|\delta T/T|\ll1$. For short wavelengths the two are equal and opposite. For
long wavelengths ($x\gtrsim1$) the density is nearly frozen while $\delta T/T$
grows by up to a factor $\sim45$ during cooling (Section~\ref{sec:isochoric}):
a seed of $\drho=0.02$ produces $\delta T/T\approx0.9$ near the end of cooling.
The temperature perturbation then goes nonlinear first, and the threshold seed
amplitude must be set by it (Figure~\ref{fig:minamp}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Validation and the long-time evolution}
\label{sec:validation}
Periodic gPLUTO runs with $A=10^{-4}$ match the linear theory through the whole
cooling history and for 200 cooling times afterwards, for $x=0.1$, 1 and 10, with
a floor and with the UV background (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}). The five seed
types of Section~\ref{sec:seeds} match equally well (Figure~\ref{fig:perthist}).

\label{fig:validation}

\paragraph{Do the perturbations eventually go away?} Once all the gas has reached
the floor (or $\Teq$), the temperature perturbation is erased, but the density
perturbation is not. It is no longer supported by a temperature difference, so it
turns into a sound wave of the cold gas, with period $\lambda/c_{s,\rm cold}$
($c_{s,\rm cold}\approx15$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, ten times smaller than initially). What
happens next depends on the wavelength and on the thermal model
(Figure~\ref{fig:validation}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Short wavelengths} ($x=0.1$): the growth peaks at $\sim100$ within
  $1$--$3\,\tcool$. With a hard floor the resulting sound wave persists, decaying
  only slowly (to $\sim50$ by $10\,\tcool$ and $\sim20$ by $300\,\tcool$). With the
  UV background it damps away within $\sim10$ cooling times (below the seed amplitude by
  $\sim15\,\tcool$ and $\sim10^{-2}$ of it by $30\,\tcool$): gas at $\Teq$ is thermally stable, so compressions
  radiate away their excess heat out of phase with the wave.
\item \emph{Intermediate wavelengths} ($x=1$): the density keeps growing after
  cooling, driven by the velocity imprinted during cooling, and peaks at $\sim35$
  after $10$--$20\,\tcool$, about a quarter of the cold sound-crossing time. It then
  decays slowly; with the UV background it is gone by $\sim1000\,\tcool$.
\item \emph{Long wavelengths} ($x=10$): the density grows only slowly, to $\sim5$
  after $50\,\tcool$, and stays at 3--5 for longer than the Hubble time.
\end{itemize}
So the perturbations do eventually go away, but only through damping of the
post-cooling sound waves, and only at short wavelengths within a Hubble time.
For $n=10^{-3}$ and the UV background $\tcool=320$\,Myr, so the Hubble time is only
$44\,\tcool$; for $n=10^{-2}$ with the floor it is $680\,\tcool$. In the nonlinear
runs shown later, the sound waves also steepen into shocks and dissipate.

\subsection{Growth versus $\lambda/(\cstc)$ in linear theory}
\label{sec:growthx}
\textbf{Figures~\ref{fig:linear}--\ref{fig:minamp} and Table~\ref{tab:inf} are
linear theory only} (numerical integration of
Eqs.~\ref{eq:s}--\ref{eq:bg}; no simulations). The infinite-medium amplification
falls from $\sim70$ while cooling at $x=0.03$ to nothing at $x\gtrsim10$. A small
isobaric cloud amplifies far more, $\sim10^3$ while cooling at $x\lesssim0.1$, because
compression raises the exponent in Eq.~(\ref{eq:timeshift}) from $1/\gamma$ to 1, but
this limit is hard to reach (Section~\ref{sec:backgrounds}). Metallicity enters mostly through
$\cstc$; at fixed $x$, $Z=0.05$ and $Z=1$ differ by $\lesssim30\%$. Starting cooler
leaves less of the cooling curve to traverse: from $2.5\times10^5$\,K the
infinite-medium amplification is $\lesssim25$ (Figure~\ref{fig:linearT0}).

threshold at $x\gtrsim1$ drops to MINAMPT, because $\delta T/T$ reaches order
unity long before $\drho$ does.

\subsection{The cooling switch and numerical mixing}
\label{sec:mixing}
The tracer that switches cooling on is advected with the gas, so the cooling
region moves inward with the cloud as it contracts and hot gas flowing in behind
it does not cool. The only non-Lagrangian part is numerical diffusion at the
cloud edge: cells there contain a mix of cloud and background gas and cool
partially. The edge stays sharp (4--8 mixed cells), but once a cloud has
collapsed, hot gas flowing onto the cold slab is mixed into it numerically and
cools. Table~\ref{tab:mask} tests this on a collapsing cloud.

The growth while cooling is unaffected. The leakage appears only after collapse;
a sharp switch barely helps, and it decreases only slowly with resolution. Across
the cloud set it is 2--3\% for large clouds, 7--11\% for $L\approx5\,\cstc$ and up
to $\sim40\%$ for clouds that collapse to a few cells. This is a limitation of any
fixed-grid code; a switch based on a density threshold would be worse, because
compressed hot gas crosses the threshold. In 1D the clean solution is a
Lagrangian (mass-coordinate) code, in which the cooling switch is exactly
attached to the gas and a collapsing cloud keeps its resolution automatically.

\subsection{The long-wavelength (isochoric) regime}
\label{sec:isochoric}
For $\lambda\gg\cstc$ each parcel cools at its own fixed density. The cooling rate
per particle is $\propto n\Lambda(T)$, so denser parcels run ahead in temperature:
$\delta T/T=\delta p/p$ grows $\approx45$ times during cooling, independent of
$\lambda$, while the density does not change (Figure~\ref{fig:isochoric}). That
pressure pattern pushes gas from the hotter, underdense parcels into the colder
ones. During the cooling time the gas moves only $\sim(\cstc/\lambda)^2$ of a
wavelength, but the velocity persists after cooling, so the density keeps
growing. All 26 clouds with $x\ge1.5$ and $L\ge1.5\,\cstc$, with
$x$ from 1.7 to 230, follow
\begin{equation}
G-1\approx24\left(\frac{\cstc}{\lambda}\right)^2\quad\text{by }2.5\,\tcool
\label{eq:isoscaling}
\end{equation}
(10--90\% range of the prefactor: 20--29; median 24 with the floor and 28 with
the UV background). This is the scaling expected for a pressure impulse of order
$\delta T/T$ acting for $\sim\tcool$ across a wavelength. The growth continues
for a good fraction of the time sound takes to cross $\lambda$ in the cold gas,
$\lambda/c_{s,\rm cold}\approx17x\,\tcool$ (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}); our
cloud runs stop at $3\,\tcool$. If $\delta T/T$ reaches order
unity during cooling, which here happens for $A\gtrsim0.02$, parts of the cloud
reach the floor while their neighbours are still hot and are crushed; this is
the isochoric fragmentation picture of \citet{mccourt18}.

\subsection{Photoionizing UV background}
\label{sec:uvb}
The UV background suppresses cooling below $\sim10^5$\,K, strongly at low density
(at $n=10^{-4}$ the $10^5$\,K peak is roughly halved and the hydrogen peak near
$2\times10^4$\,K disappears), and replaces the floor by a density-dependent
$\Teq$ (Figure~\ref{fig:coolmodels}). Because $\Teq$ falls with density,
compressed gas is cooler than its surroundings at equilibrium, which makes it
easier to compress than gas held at a fixed floor. The UV background also breaks
the exact density scaling of collisional cooling, though only weakly.

What grows is the entropy (cooling-time) perturbation
(Table~\ref{tab:seedgrowth}, Figure~\ref{fig:pertlinear}):
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{temperature} seed carries $-1/\gamma$ times the entropy perturbation
  of a density seed and gives $\sim0.6$ times its growth, with the opposite sign:
  hot spots end up underdense;
\item a \emph{metallicity} seed changes the cooling rate (metals carry $\sim88\%$
  of the cooling at $10^6$\,K for $Z=0.3$) and gives $\sim0.35$ times the growth of
  a density seed. Metal-rich gas ends up denser, and because $Z$ stays with the
  gas the forcing never switches off;
\item \emph{sound waves} and \emph{velocity} seeds carry no entropy. Cooling
  converts some of their compression into entropy, giving 10--40 times less
  growth than a density seed at short wavelengths. At long wavelengths the
  velocity seed compresses the gas directly and matters about as much as a
  density seed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Do perturbations always grow?}
During cooling a density seed never fell below its initial amplitude in any
infinite-medium run starting from $10^5$--$10^6$\,K. In linear theory it can only
shrink where $\LT>2$, which on these curves happens only between about
$2\times10^4$ and $6\times10^4$\,K. After the gas reaches the floor or $\Teq$ the
perturbation becomes a sound wave in the cold gas (Section~\ref{sec:validation}):
\begin{itemize}
\item at short wavelengths ($x\lesssim0.3$) it swings through zero every half
  period. With the UV background it damps away within $\sim10$--$30\,\tcool$; with a
  hard floor it persists;
\item at $x\gtrsim1$ the density keeps growing long after cooling, for a good fraction of
  the cold sound-crossing time $\approx17x\,\tcool$;
\item small clouds smooth out after collapsing (Figure~\ref{fig:amplitude}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Comments on the poster setup}
\label{sec:critique}
%======================================================================
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Measure $\lambda$ and $L$ in units of $\cstc$ of the cloud gas.} With
  a small contrast the cloud gas is hot and $\cstc$ is large: for $\chi=1$ at
  $n=10^{-2}$, $\cstc=5$\,kpc, and $\propto1/n$. The poster's $\lambda\approx7$--30\,kpc
  at $n\approx0.01$--0.02 then corresponds to $x\sim1$--5: modest growth while
  cooling and most of it afterwards, consistent with what she measured.
\item \textbf{The diagnostic.} Her central/interior maximum is the right idea.
  Measuring in the mass coordinate (the cloud shrinks) and projecting onto the
  seeded mode (to separate it from harmonics and from waves launched at the edges)
  are two refinements.
\item \textbf{Comparing runs at a fixed simulation time.} $\tcool$ differs by large
  factors between runs. Normalize time by when the gas actually cools
  (Section~\ref{sec:tnorm}), not by when it reaches the floor exactly.
\item \textbf{Use dimensionless parameters}: $\lambda/(\cstc)$, $L/(\cstc)$, $Z$
  (weakly) and $A$. With collisional cooling, density and size are exactly
  degenerate (Section~\ref{sec:clouds}).
\item \textbf{Units and run length.} The poster states 0.1 code units = 0.01\,Gyr,
  but the profiles run to $\sim0.1$\,Gyr; with kpc and 100\,km\,s$^{-1}$ units one
  code time unit is 9.78\,Myr. The runs stop around 1--2\,$\tcool$, before most of
  the post-cooling growth.
\item \textbf{Pressure equilibrium holds only at $t=0$.} Once the cloud cools it
  is out of pressure balance with the hot gas and edge effects reach the interior.
  The periodic infinite medium avoids this.
\item \textbf{Background thermal model.} Switching cooling on only in cloud gas
  works well; a tracer is cleaner than a density threshold (Section~\ref{sec:switch}).
\item \textbf{Resolution.} A cloud smaller than $\cstc$ is compressed 100--400 times,
  so cells per wavelength must be counted after the collapse.
\item \textbf{Linearity.} For $x\gtrsim1$ the temperature perturbation goes nonlinear
  long before the density does (Figure~\ref{fig:minamp}); a 2\% seed is already
  nonlinear in temperature.
\item \textbf{Single sinusoid, single quantity.} The kind of perturbation matters as
  much as its amplitude (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}). A spectrum with random phases in
  several fields is the natural next step.
\item \textbf{Cooling at low density.} A UV background is the better default for CGM
  densities. It changes the growth while cooling only modestly but damps the
  post-cooling sound waves.
\item \textbf{1D limits.} Cold gas can only form slabs, and at a uniform floor
  temperature and pressure the slabs end at the same density. ``Shattering''
  needs 2D or 3D; the 1D survey tells you which ($x$, $L/\cstc$, $A$, seed type) to
  run there.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The original cooling curve is an approximation (0.1--0.3\,dex off between
  $2\times10^5$ and $3\times10^6$\,K); the Cloudy runs give the same trends.
\item The UV-background runs use $z=0$ only, keep the mean molecular weight of fully
  ionized gas (so $T$ is slightly misplaced near $10^4$\,K), and ignore self-shielding.
\item The behaviour after cooling with a floor depends on how the floor is
  implemented (here a smooth cutoff, $1-1/\cosh[(T/\Tfl)^{12}]$).
\item The PLUTO~4.4 port of the setup in the repository was not compiled.
\item Late-time values for strongly compressed small clouds are resolution-limited
  (Section~\ref{sec:resolution}).
\end{itemize}

\appendix
\section{Changes from earlier versions}
\label{app:corrections}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Only $\chi=1$.} Earlier drafts used clouds denser than the
  background, mostly $\chi=2$. All cloud results are now for $\chi=1$; the
  amplitude, resolution, seed-type and long-wavelength sets were rerun.
\item \textbf{Comparison with a heated medium.} An earlier draft compared the
  amplification of cooling and heated gas over the same elapsed time. Since the
  background of cooling gas changes by a large factor, the fair comparison is per
  e-fold of cooling (Section~\ref{sec:whyheated}).
\item \textbf{Time normalization.} Earlier versions ended ``cooling'' when the gas
  reached $1.05\,\Tfl$ or $1.05\,\Teq$. With the corrected definition the growth
  while cooling is about two times smaller at $x\lesssim0.3$ and the prefactor of
  Eq.~(\ref{eq:isoscaling}) is $\approx25$ rather than 50.
\item \textbf{UV background.} An earlier version said the UV background roughly
  halves the growth while cooling at $x\approx0.3$--3. That came from the slow final
  approach to the floor in the Cloudy CIE table inflating its end-of-cooling time;
  with consistent times the two agree to $\sim20\%$.
\item \textbf{Diagnostic.} An early version said the poster's measurement was
  dominated by the cloud edges; the student measures the interior, which is sound.
\end{itemize}
